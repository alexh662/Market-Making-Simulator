# Decisions

## 2026-08-11 Arithmetic vs geometric Brownian motion
**Decision:** Mid-price follows arithmetic Brownian motion with no drift, `S_{t+dt} = S_t + sigma * sqrt(dt) * Z`.
**Alternatives considered:** Geometric Brownian motion (log-normal price, proportional volatility).
**Reasoning:** CLAUDE.md section 2.2 mandates arithmetic BM as the Avellaneda-Stoikov convention; the closed-form quoting solution (reservation price, optimal spread) is derived under arithmetic BM and does not carry over cleanly to GBM. Arithmetic dynamics also keep inventory PnL linear in price increments, which is what the PnL decomposition in section 5 relies on.
**Consequence:** Prices can in principle go negative over long horizons or high sigma, but at the frozen defaults (S0=100, sigma=2.0, T=1.0) this is not a practical concern. No drift is added in the base case so PnL variance can be attributed to inventory risk rather than a directional bet; drift is reserved as an optional robustness check (section 6.7).

## 2026-08-11 Config validation rules
**Decision:** Config is represented as frozen dataclasses (`PriceConfig`, `FlowConfig`, `StrategyConfig`, `SimulationConfig`, `Config`) that validate their own fields in `__post_init__`, raising `ValueError` on: `sigma <= 0`, `dt <= 0`, `T <= 0`, `kappa <= 0`, `gamma <= 0`, `tick_size <= 0`, `n_paths < 1`, `phi_informed` outside `[0, 1]`, and `T / dt` not within `1e-9` of an integer.
**Alternatives considered:** A single flat config dataclass; validating with a schema library (e.g. `pydantic`); validating only at the point of use rather than at construction.
**Reasoning:** CLAUDE.md section 13 requires frozen dataclasses for config (no dictionaries passed as configuration) and forbids adding dependencies without logging them here — `pydantic` was not added for that reason. Validating in `__post_init__` means an invalid config fails immediately at construction, whether built from YAML or directly in tests, rather than surfacing as a confusing downstream numerical error (e.g. a `dt` that doesn't evenly divide `T` would silently produce a path one element short or long). The `T / dt` integer check uses a `1e-9` tolerance because floating-point division (e.g. `1.0 / 0.005`) is not exact.
**Consequence:** Each config section validates independently of the others, which is sufficient since none of the required rules are cross-section. If a future rule needs to compare fields across sections (e.g. price vs strategy), it will need to move into `Config.__post_init__` instead.

## 2026-08-11 Simulator internals accept raw parameters, not a validated Config
**Decision:** `run_episode` in `src/simulator.py` takes individual keyword parameters (`sigma`, `A`, `phi_informed`, etc.) rather than a `Config` object, and performs no validation of its own.
**Alternatives considered:** Passing a `Config` object straight through to `run_episode`; relaxing `PriceConfig`'s `sigma > 0` check to allow the section 8 sanity check.
**Reasoning:** CLAUDE.md section 8's mandatory sanity checks require running the simulator with `sigma = 0` and `A = 0`, but section 12's calibration note and the earlier config milestone both require `PriceConfig` to reject `sigma <= 0` for real experiment configs. Routing every call through the validated `Config` would make the two requirements mutually exclusive. `Config`/`load_config` remains the strict boundary for anything loaded from YAML (section 13's "no dictionaries passed as configuration" applies there); `run_episode` is an internal function, trusted per the project's style guide ("only validate at system boundaries"), so it accepts whatever the caller passes, including the degenerate test parameterisations.
**Consequence:** Callers driving `run_episode` from a real `Config` (e.g. `experiments.py`, not yet written) must unpack its fields explicitly rather than passing the object. `sigma = 0` and `A = 0` are valid simulator inputs for tests even though `config/base.yaml` could never validate to those values.

## 2026-08-11 Informed-flow mechanism
**Decision:** Each step, ordinary per-side fill draws happen exactly as in section 2.6 (independent Bernoulli per side from the Poisson intensity), unconditioned on informed status. Any arrival that does fill is then reclassified informed with probability `phi_informed`. An informed bid fill (someone selling into us) only executes if the mid is about to fall over the next `informed_horizon` steps; an informed ask fill only executes if the mid is about to rise. An informed arrival on the wrong side is cancelled outright rather than flipped to the other side.
**Alternatives considered:** Overriding the side of an informed arrival to force it onto the profitable side regardless of the independent per-side draw; adding a separate, additive informed-arrival process on top of the uninformed one.
**Reasoning:** CLAUDE.md section 2.7 says the informed order's "direction is chosen to be correct," but section 2.6 already fixes fill mechanics as two independent per-side draws, and overriding the side after the fact would silently violate the stated distance-decay fill probabilities. Gating (cancel-if-wrong) preserves the section 2.6 mechanics exactly, keeps `phi_informed` as a clean fraction of raw arrivals, and yields the intended effect: as `phi_informed` rises, more of our fills are will-be-informed and a smaller fraction of those informed attempts survive being on the wrong side, so filled flow is pushed toward the mid's true direction. This directly produces the negative adverse-selection cost the decomposition in section 5.2 measures.
**Consequence:** Realised fill counts fall somewhat as `phi_informed` rises (informed-but-wrong arrivals vanish rather than converting to a same-side uninformed fill), which is a deliberate and documented side effect, not a bug — it must be accounted for when reading the fill-count column in the informed-flow sweep (section 6.6).

## 2026-08-11 FillRecord horizon field uses adverse_selection_horizon, not informed_horizon
**Decision:** `FillRecord.mid_after_horizon` is the mid price `adverse_selection_horizon` steps after the fill, not `informed_horizon` steps after. The `informed_horizon`-ahead price used to classify a fill as informed (section 2.7) is consumed internally by the simulator and only surfaces via `was_informed`; it is not stored as its own column.
**Alternatives considered:** Storing both horizons as separate columns; storing only the `informed_horizon` price since that is what section 2.7 names first.
**Reasoning:** Section 4.3 requires everything downstream (section 5.2's adverse-selection decomposition) to read from the recorded frames without re-running the simulation, and section 5.2's formula explicitly uses `h = adverse_selection_horizon`. Both horizons default to 20 in `config/base.yaml`, which makes the two choices numerically identical today, but they are conceptually distinct knobs (one governs the counterparty model, the other governs how PnL is attributed) and only one of them is what `decomposition.py` will need.
**Consequence:** If `informed_horizon` and `adverse_selection_horizon` are ever set to different values, `mid_after_horizon` still reflects the attribution horizon, and `was_informed` reflects the (unrecorded) classification horizon — the two can diverge, which is intentional.

## 2026-08-11 StateRecord bid/ask/spread are post-rounding; reservation_price is pre-rounding
**Decision:** In `StateRecord`, `bid`/`ask`/`spread_total` are the tick-rounded, actually-tradeable quotes. `reservation_price` is `(raw_bid + raw_ask) / 2` computed from the strategy's raw output before tick rounding.
**Alternatives considered:** Recording everything post-rounding; recording everything pre-rounding; extending the `Strategy.quote()` interface to return a reservation price explicitly.
**Reasoning:** The `Strategy` protocol in section 6.1 returns only `tuple[float, float]`, and CLAUDE.md is the source of truth for that interface, so it was not extended. Section 2.5 states quotes are placed symmetrically about the reservation price, which means `(bid + ask) / 2` recovers it exactly for any conforming strategy, without needing a dedicated field. Post-rounding is used for `bid`/`ask`/`spread_total` because those are what a counterparty actually trades against and what section 4.2 means by "average quoted spread" for the liquidation cost; pre-rounding is used for `reservation_price` because that is the model quantity (`r_t` in section 2.3), not a market-facing price.
**Consequence:** `spread_total` in the state frame is the realised, tick-rounded spread, which can be very slightly wider than `2 * |reservation_price - mid|` implies before rounding; this gap is the rounding-collision counter's effect and is expected, not an error.

## 2026-08-11 Added strategy.fixed_spread config key
**Decision:** Added `strategy.fixed_spread: 1.0` to `config/base.yaml` and `StrategyConfig`, validated `> 0`. This is a new key, not a change to an existing default.
**Alternatives considered:** Hardcoding the baseline spread inside `SymmetricFixedSpread` or in test code; deriving it from the time-averaged Avellaneda-Stoikov spread now (per section 16's guidance for the headline comparison).
**Reasoning:** `SymmetricFixedSpread` needs a spread width and the project's config style forbids passing bare numbers instead of named config (section 13). `1.0` was chosen so the baseline produces roughly 100-400 fills per 200-step episode at the frozen defaults (observed mean ~102 fills over 100 paths), matching the calibration target in section 12. Setting it to the time-averaged AS spread, as section 16 recommends for the headline comparison, is deferred until the AS strategy exists (M3) and can be measured; `1.0` is a placeholder good enough for M2's simulator tests.
**Consequence:** This value will likely be revisited in M5 (headline comparison) once the AS strategy's time-averaged spread is known, per section 16.

## 2026-08-12 Total spread is not monotonic in gamma in general
**Decision:** `test_total_spread_increases_in_gamma_at_frozen_default_parameters` checks monotonicity only across the section 6.3 gamma sweep `[0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0]`, at `t=0` (`T-t=T=1.0`), with the frozen default `sigma=2.0`, `kappa=1.5`. The Avellaneda-Stoikov formula itself was not changed to force monotonicity.
**Alternatives considered:** Asserting monotonicity in gamma unconditionally (fails); clipping or otherwise modifying the spread formula so it is monotonic everywhere (would depart from the closed-form solution in section 2.4, which is not permitted).
**Reasoning:** `spread_total(gamma) = gamma*sigma^2*(T-t) + (2/gamma)*ln(1+gamma/kappa)` has two terms with opposite gamma-dependence: the inventory term is linear-increasing in gamma, the microstructure term `(2/gamma)*ln(1+gamma/kappa)` is decreasing in gamma for large enough gamma. At the frozen defaults with `T-t=1.0`, the inventory term's increase dominates across the whole sweep (spread rises from 1.369 at gamma=0.01 to 20.587 at gamma=5.0, monotonically). But near the episode horizon this breaks down: at `T-t=0.1`, spread at gamma=0.01 is 1.333 and at gamma=0.05 is 1.332 — a decrease. The counter-example was found by hand calculation, not by search, so it is a real, reproducible property of the formula, not a numerical artefact.
**Consequence:** Any future code (e.g. `experiments.py`'s gamma sweep, section 6.3) that assumes total spread is monotonic in gamma must restrict that assumption to conditions similar to the frozen defaults at `t` near 0. Reporting the gamma sweep's Sharpe peak (section 6.3) should not conflate "total spread is smallest at low gamma" with a universal property.

## 2026-08-12 Mid-crossing counted, not clipped
**Decision:** `EpisodeResult.mid_crossing_count` counts every step where the strategy's raw (pre-tick-rounding) bid is above the mid or raw ask is below the mid. The simulator does not clip, clamp, or otherwise prevent this; it only counts it.
**Alternatives considered:** Clamping the quote to never cross the mid; silently ignoring the condition (the default absent this change); raising an exception.
**Reasoning:** At large `|q|`, the reservation-price shift `q*gamma*sigma^2*(T-t)` can exceed half the total spread, pushing both quotes to the same side of the true mid — e.g. at defaults, `q=3` and `t=0` is already enough (shift 1.2 vs half-spread 0.845). This is a real, reproducible artefact of the closed-form AS solution at extreme inventory, not a bug, and altering the formula to prevent it would depart from CLAUDE.md section 2.4. Since it is common enough at default parameters (observed on several of the first ten Monte Carlo paths), it needs to be visible rather than silently baked into fill behaviour.
**Consequence:** A mid-crossed quote is still tradeable exactly as any other quote (the fill-probability formula in section 2.6 does not special-case it): a crossed ask, being below the true mid, gets a very high fill probability, which mechanically pulls inventory back toward zero. This is a real, useful self-correcting property of the model worth noting in the M5/M6 write-up, not something to engineer away.

## 2026-08-12 quote_skew_example.png uses gamma=0.01 and seed=53
**Decision:** `scripts/make_figures.py` generates `results/figures/quote_skew_example.png` using `AvellanedaStoikov(gamma=0.01, sigma=2.0, kappa=1.5, T=1.0)` and `np.random.default_rng(53)`, reaching `|inventory| = 15` at `t=0.465`. All other parameters (S0, sigma, dt, T, A, kappa, order_size, phi_informed, informed_horizon, tick_size, adverse_selection_horizon, liquidation_cost_multiplier) are the frozen defaults from `config/base.yaml`.
**Alternatives considered:** At the time this entry was first written, the frozen default was `gamma=0.1`; the alternative considered then was using that default and searching for a seed reaching `|inventory| >= 15`.
**Reasoning:** This entry originally justified deviating from the frozen default `gamma=0.1`, where AS's own restoring force is strong by design: a search over 20,000 seeds found a maximum of `|q|=12` (seed 3537), never reaching 15. Since then the frozen default itself was lowered to `gamma=0.01` (see the "gamma default lowered to 0.01" entry), which is the same value this figure was already using — so `QUOTE_SKEW_GAMMA` in `scripts/make_figures.py` is no longer a deviation, it now coincides with the frozen default. `seed=53` is still a deliberate, non-default choice: not every seed at `gamma=0.01` reaches `|inventory| >= 15` (most don't), so it remains a cherry-picked seed for visual legibility, not a representative one.
**Consequence:** The figure is now, coincidentally, generated at the actual frozen default gamma rather than an off-default one, though `scripts/make_figures.py` still hardcodes the value (`QUOTE_SKEW_GAMMA = 0.01`) independently of `config/base.yaml` rather than reading it, and its own comment still describes gamma=0.01 as chosen "over the frozen default gamma=0.1" — that comment is now stale and should be corrected if the script is next touched. The seed remains non-representative of typical inventory behaviour at the frozen defaults (M5/M6 sweep figures, e.g. `inventory_paths.png`, are what show that); this entry and the figure's caption record why a specific seed, not a typical one, was chosen.

## 2026-08-12 gamma default lowered to 0.01
**Decision:** Default `gamma` changed from `0.1` to `0.01`.
**Alternatives considered:** Keeping `0.1`, or going below `0.001`.
**Reasoning:** At `gamma=0.1` the inventory standard deviation was `1.54` with max absolute inventory `11`, too tight for the inventory management problem to be visible in the AS versus fixed spread comparison. At `gamma=0.01` the inventory standard deviation is `3.57` with max absolute inventory `19`, mid crossings remain at zero, and fill counts stay healthy at roughly `81` per episode.
**Consequence:** The frozen defaults now produce a visible inventory problem, and the gamma sweep is recentred on the usable band.

## 2026-08-12 mid crossing is non monotonic in gamma
**Decision:** No code change; recording an empirical finding for later use.
**Alternatives considered:** N/A — this is an observation, not a design choice.
**Reasoning:** Mid crossings per episode are near zero for `gamma` at or below `0.01`, peak at roughly `19.6` per episode at `gamma=0.5`, then fall at `gamma=5.0` because fill counts collapse to about `12` per episode, leaving few opportunities to cross. The peak is not at the extreme of the parameter range in either direction — it is driven by two competing effects (skew magnitude rising with gamma, fill count falling with gamma) that trade off in the middle.
**Consequence:** Candidate finding for the README: mid-crossing frequency is a distinct, non-monotonic diagnostic from inventory variance or PnL, worth its own sentence in the write-up rather than being folded into the gamma sweep narrative.
